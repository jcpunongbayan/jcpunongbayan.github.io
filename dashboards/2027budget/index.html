National expenditure program

Proposed 2027 budget
The real score

by JC Punongbayan

Every August, the President submits the National Expenditure Program (NEP), or his proposed budget, to Congress. By December, Congress passes the General Appropriations Act (GAA). The figures that change between the two documents show where Congress actually intervened. I compared nine budget documents from 2023 to 2027.

01 · Scale The 2027 proposal in full

PHP 7.2 trillion, drawn one billion at a time

Each square is PHP 1 billion. The grid has 7,200 squares, while the full proposal is PHP 0.186 billion larger. PHP 1 million is one thousandth of a square.

Written out in full, the 2027 proposal is PHP 7,200,186,000,000.

In more familiar terms, that is PHP 7,200 billion, or PHP 7.2 trillion.

The grid draws each of those billions as one square. A million pesos would be a thousandth of one square, too small to print on any page.

To get a sense of the scale, a million seconds is 11.6 days, while a billion seconds is 31.7 years. At one peso per second, PHP 7.2 trillion would last about 228,000 years.

But divide the budget by the population and it comes to roughly PHP 63,000 per Filipino for the year, or a little over PHP 170 a day for schools, roads, hospitals, soldiers, and debt.

02 · Composition The 2027 proposal

Congress cannot touch 
about two-fifths of the budget

Automatic appropriationProgrammed

The 13 biggest items in the 2027 proposal, out of 103. Each ordinary department is shown as a single total. The large automatic and lump-sum funds are broken into their parts.

About two-fifths of the proposed 2027 budget is already spoken for. Local governments' share of national taxes and interest on the national debt alone come to PHP 2,434 billion.

That is already 33.8% of the entire proposal.

Nobody sits down each year and decides those two amounts. One is a share of taxes collected three years earlier, fixed by law. The other is whatever we owe our lenders.

Add the BARMM block grant (the yearly transfer to the Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao), pensions, and the retirement premiums of government workers. About 40% of the budget is spoken for before anyone chooses anything.

But the budget fight is over what remains. Education and public works alone take 22.5% of the total.

03 · Size Budget against the economy, 2023–2027

The budget barely moves 
as a share of the economy

Measured economyForecast economy

Total appropriations as a % of nominal gross domestic product. GDP for 2023 to 2025 is the actual outturn from the Philippine Statistics Authority. GDP for 2026 and 2027 is the low end of the range the government projected alongside the 2027 proposal, which is the basis it used for its own published 21.7% figure.

A budget of PHP 7.2 trillion sounds enormous. But set against the size of the economy, it is almost exactly what it was four years ago.

The budget-GDP ratio has sat between 21.7% and 22.6% of gross domestic product (GDP) in every year since 2023.

The peak is 2025, at 22.6%. That was not a spending surge. The budget grew 9.7% that year while the economy grew 5.9% in peso terms, so the same money became a larger slice of a smaller economy than anyone had planned for.

But the last two bars use forecast gross domestic product. Recall that the government has missed its growth targets before.

For 2025 the government projected nominal GDP of PHP 28,356 billion at the low end of its range. The outturn was PHP 28,014 billion, short by PHP 342 billion.

The advertised 21.7% for 2027 assumes the economy grows 8.8% in peso terms. Miss by as much as 2025 missed, and the ratio is 22.0% instead.

Which budget

This counts appropriations, meaning the spending authority written into the budget documents. The government's headline figures usually count disbursements, the cash that actually goes out, which is lower. For 2026 it reports disbursements at 21.2% of GDP against the 22.3% shown here. Both are right. They are not the same measure.

04 · Measurement Proposal vs law, 2023–2026

Congress changes programs, 
not agency totals

How much of the budget Congress moves. Read each bar this way: that share of every peso would have to change hands to turn the President's proposal into the law that was signed. The same question, asked at four levels of detail.

Look only at department totals and Congress seems to move about 6% of the budget. But look at individual programs and the figure rises to 16–23%.

In short, roughly three quarters of the money Congress moves stays in the same department. It is taken from one program and given to another.

Judge the budget by department totals and all of that is invisible.

Going finer still, down to individual funds and expense types, barely changes the answer.

The real bargaining happens at the level of programs.

Worth knowing

Congress cannot make the budget bigger. The Constitution caps it at the President's total. So everything here is money moved from one purpose to another, never money added.

05 · Persistence Four years, four handovers

The President takes back most 
of Congress's budget changes

Each dot is one year's survival rate, which I call lambda (λ). The grey line through it is the margin of error. A value of 1.0 would mean the President's next proposal fully accepts what Congress enacted. A value of 0 would mean he ignores it completely and starts again from his own earlier figure.

After Congress passes one budget, the President writes the next proposal. How much of Congress's changes does he keep?

The answer, every year, is between 30% and 50%. Raise a program by PHP 10 billion and the next proposal typically keeps only PHP 3 to PHP 5 billion of that increase. The rest is quietly pulled back toward what the President asked for in the first place.

I call that survival rate lambda (λ). It is a share rather than a peso amount, so we can compare it across four very different budgets.

Put differently, the President's previous request carries roughly twice the weight of the law that was actually signed.

Confidential funds at the Department of the Interior and Local Government (DILG) show it plainly. They were proposed at exactly PHP 0.907 billion 4 years running, raised by Congress three times out of four, and proposed again unchanged each time.

What lambda misses

This tracks whether the pesos survive, not whether the projects do. A program can keep the same funding level from one year to the next while the specific roads, schools, or beneficiaries inside it change entirely.

06 · Reversion 2026 proposal → 2026 law → 2027 proposal

The 2027 proposal reverses most 
of Congress's 2026 changes

Congress changed itThe 2027 proposal changed it back

Change in funding, PHP billion. Opposite bars mean the 2027 proposal undoes Congress's change; bars in the same direction mean it goes further. AICS is Assistance to Individuals in Crisis Situations, AKAP is Ayuda sa Kapos ang Kita Program, TUPAD is the labor department's emergency employment program, PAFF is Presidential Assistance to Farmers and Fisherfolk, and 4Ps is Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program.

Take the Department of Health's medical assistance program. Its budget goes from PHP 24.24 billion in the 2026 proposal to PHP 51.65 billion in the law, then back to PHP 24.24 billion in the 2027 proposal.

The same pattern repeats across the assistance programs. Of the PHP 500 billion Congress added across government, PHP 283 billion is taken out again in the next proposal. Of what it cut, PHP 306 billion is put back.

But the Local Government Support Fund (LGSF) is the exception. Congress raised it by PHP 41.8 billion and the President kept the higher figure. It is the only large fund on this list where what Congress enacted became the new starting point.

Note that a year earlier the same fund was raised and then pulled back down.

For now, this is a break in the pattern, not yet a new rule.

07 · Ayuda Cash and in-kind assistance, 2023–2027

Congress enlarged ayuda 
four years in a row

ProposedEnacted

The three biggest discretionary assistance programs added together, PHP billion. Blue figures show how much Congress added. AICS is the Assistance to Individuals in Crisis Situations, counted with AKAP because the two share one program code. MAIFIP is Medical Assistance to Indigent and Financially-Incapacitated Patients. TUPAD is the labor department’s emergency employment program.

In all four years we can compare, Congress enacted more ayuda than the President proposed.

And the gap is large. The enacted amount runs from 1.5 to 2.6 times the proposal.

Congress added PHP 31.8 billion in 2023, PHP 92.6 billion in 2024, PHP 53.5 billion in 2025, and PHP 75.0 billion in 2026.

But look at the Pantawid Pamilyang Pilipino Program (4Ps). It is our largest cash transfer and the one with the tightest rules on who qualifies. In the 2025 law, Congress cut it from PHP 114.2 billion to PHP 64.2 billion.

Note where the missing PHP 50 billion went. It reappears in the 2025 list of unprogrammed appropriations, or spending released only if extra revenue comes in, at exactly PHP 50 billion.

So in one budget, Congress raised the assistance that officials hand out case by case, and made the rules-based transfer conditional on money that may never arrive.

What we cannot tell

These programs are also our main emergency safety net. More money could mean patronage, but it could also mean that more people received help.

The budget documents cannot tell us which is true. For that, we would need beneficiary-level data, and the government does not publish them.

08 · Local funds Transfers to local governments, 2023–2027

Congress more than tripled the 
Local Government Support Fund

ProposedEnacted

The Local Government Support Fund (LGSF) in each of the nine budget documents, PHP billion. Blue figures show how much Congress added. In 2023 Congress cut the fund instead, by PHP 12.4 billion.

Four big streams carry national money to local governments. Three of them are set by formula, and Congress has never changed any of the three by a single peso.

The National Tax Allotment, the special shares of local governments in national taxes, and the BARMM block grant are identical in the proposal and in the law, in all four years.

Only the fourth stream has no formula. The Local Government Support Fund (LGSF) is a lump sum, and it is the one that moves.

In the 2026 budget it went from PHP 16.1 billion proposed to PHP 57.9 billion enacted. That is a rise of 260% in one passage through Congress.

But almost all of the increase is in one line. Financial Assistance to Local Government Units (FALGU), which has no allocation rule, went from PHP 5.0 billion to PHP 37.5 billion. The Growth Equity Fund (GEF) added another PHP 10.3 billion.

Note that the President kept the higher figure. The LGSF is proposed at PHP 58.5 billion for 2027, with Financial Assistance to LGUs held at exactly PHP 37.5 billion.

The 2023 exception

Congress does not always add. In 2023 it cut the LGSF from PHP 28.9 billion to PHP 16.5 billion. The 2026 jump breaks the earlier pattern, but one year is not enough to establish a new one.

09 · Public works Public works, 2023–2027

Flood control held flat for 
3 years, then fell 98% in 2026

ProposedEnacted

Flood control funding at the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH), in each of the nine budget documents from 2023 to 2027. PHP billion.

After intense public scrutiny, Congress slashed flood control in the 2026 budget. The amount fell from PHP 250.8 billion in the proposal to just PHP 4.8 billion in the law.

That is a 98% cut. By comparison, the 2025 proposal and law were PHP 254.3 billion and PHP 249.8 billion, respectively. The 2027 proposal partly restores the amount to PHP 103.5 billion.

This was not a gradual change in priorities.

But the entire cut did not stay in the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH). The department's budget fell from PHP 881.3 billion to PHP 530.9 billion, while funding for local roads, bridges, and multipurpose halls rose by only PHP 73 billion.

Most of the missing PHP 246 billion left the department altogether, for health, social welfare, and the Philippine Health Insurance Corporation (PhilHealth).

The project list

The 18,172 named flood control projects in the 2026 law were not written in by Congress. The President's proposal already contained the full list of 190,777 projects, but with zero amounts. The law merely attaches money to entries already on the list. So counting project names tells us little about who chose them.

10 · Transport Transport department, 2027 proposal

Two projects get 97% 
of the railway budget

Railway projects with money attached in the 2027 proposal, PHP billion. Seventeen more rail projects are named in the same document with nothing beside them. LRT is the Light Rail Transit, MRT the Metro Rail Transit, and PNR the Philippine National Railways.

The North–South Commuter Railway and the Metro Manila Subway take PHP 191.3 billion. That is 97% of all rail money and 64% of the entire Department of Transportation (DOTr).

Everything else on rails, meaning the whole Light Rail Transit (LRT) and Metro Rail Transit (MRT) network plus the Philippine National Railways (PNR), comes to PHP 6.3 billion between them.

About two-thirds of the funding for the two mega-projects comes from loans: PHP 56.7 billion from the Asian Development Bank (ADB) and PHP 67.7 billion from Japan.

But seventeen rail projects sit at zero, among them the Mindanao Railway, PNR South Long Haul, MRT-4, and the LRT-2 extensions.

Zero here means zero in the regular budget. Projects like these usually sit on the standby list instead, which is the next section.

11 · Standby authority The standby list, 2023–2026

Congress stopped enlarging 
unprogrammed funds in 2026

ProposedEnacted

Unprogrammed appropriations, meaning spending Congress approves in advance but which can only be released if extra revenue, a new tax, or a loan actually comes in. It sits outside the regular budget, so none of it is counted in the other figures on this page.

For three straight years, Congress added PHP 219 billion, PHP 450 billion, and PHP 373 billion to the standby list. In 2026, it added nothing.

This is where Congress has a free hand. The regular budget has a ceiling it cannot raise. The standby list does not.

In fact, the PHP 450 billion added in 2024 is nearly as large as everything Congress shifted around inside the regular budget that whole year.

But 2026 breaks the pattern.

For the first time, the signed figure came in PHP 6.6 billion below what the President proposed. That is the same year Congress moved PHP 500 billion around inside the regular budget instead.

Three possible explanations

One possible reading is that Congress shifted from standby authority to regular assistance programs. But the flood control scandal may also have changed what lawmakers could defend.

Or a weaker revenue outlook may have made standby authority less useful. The budget documents cannot tell us which explanation is right.

12 · Discretion Confidential and intelligence funds

Congress did not cut confidential 
and intelligence funds

ProposedEnacted

Confidential and intelligence funds across all departments, added together, PHP billion. The blue figure above each pair is how much Congress added.

From 2023 to 2027, Congress did not cut the combined total of confidential and intelligence funds.

2024 is where the pattern shifts. It is the only budget where confidential funds end up below the proposal, from PHP 4.86 billion down to PHP 4.11 billion, as the Office of the Vice President, the Department of Education (DepEd), and the Department of Foreign Affairs (DFA) are all zeroed out.

But in the same document, intelligence funds rise from PHP 5.28 billion to PHP 8.27 billion, almost all of it in the security agencies. The money moved from civilian offices to security ones, and the total still went up.

Note that the Office of the President has sat at PHP 4.560 billion in eight documents in a row, untouched by either branch.

That is 42% of everything proposed for 2027.

A sense of scale

These funds are about a fifth of 1% of the national budget. The attention they get is far larger than their share of the money.

Sure, that can be defended on accountability grounds, since spending nobody can audit in the normal way deserves scrutiny. But it is not an argument about fiscal weight, and I think the two should not be confused.

13 · Limits

Where the numbers come from, and where they stop

All budget figures come from the government's own documents: the President's proposals and the signed budget laws from 2023 to 2027, plus the separate volumes on unprogrammed appropriations. The gross domestic product figures in section 3 come from the Philippine Statistics Authority for 2023 to 2025 and from government projections for 2026 and 2027.

Three limits worth stating

  • "Congress" is shorthand. The difference between proposal and law combines House amendments, Senate amendments, the closed-door reconciliation between the two chambers, technical adjustments by the Department of Budget and Management (DBM), and items vetoed by the President. The documents do not show which stage produced each change.
  • Programs get renamed and reshuffled. A year-to-year comparison assumes that a program with the same name is still the same program. Usually it is. But some programs are split, merged, or renamed, so those comparisons need care.
  • A budget authorizes spending. It does not show what was actually released, awarded, or built. Much of the discretion comes after the budget is signed, when agencies decide when to release money and who gets the contracts. None of that appears in these documents.

The words used here

  • Proposal (NEP). The National Expenditure Program, or what the President asks Congress for, submitted every August.
  • The law (GAA). The General Appropriations Act, or what Congress passes and the President signs, usually in December.
  • Regular budget. Spending funded by revenue the government expects to have. Its total is capped at what the President proposed.
  • Unprogrammed appropriations. Spending approved in advance but released only if extra money turns up. No cap.
  • Lambda (λ). The share of a change made by Congress that survives into the President's next proposal.
